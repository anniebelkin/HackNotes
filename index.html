How to Get First Place in Every TryHackMe League (While Actually Learning)

The tips and strategies that took me to first place in every league at least once, without losing sight of the real goal: building skills that can get you a job.

A quick reminder: don’t lose sight of the point

I’m a completionist, so I know the urge to collect badges and watch a progress bar reach 100%. Still, you can get 10,000 points every week and it means nothing if you haven’t learned anything along the way.

Walkthroughs are fine. Using write-ups when you’re stuck or hit technical problems is okay. Copying answers just to collect points is not: the badges won’t help if you know nothing about the topics you “covered”.

Some people cheat. Don’t let it make your own progress feel worthless. And if chasing first place is hurting your learning, ignore the badges and focus on the best achievement TryHackMe offers: the skills that eventually get you a job.

First, a note on luck: sometimes you just get lucky, but higher leagues are harder because more people are willing to put in serious time.

1. How to get points

Not all rooms are equal. Some take hours for few points, others are quick and generous. Here’s what worked for me.

Quizzes

A great source of points, and some can be done from your phone.

Weekly recap quizzes

One quiz a week on that week’s content. Worth it if you’ve already studied the material.

Topic recap quizzes

At the end of each module. I skip them at first and return when I have a 50% or 100% boost active.

App quizzes

Reportedly efficient, but I haven’t tested them with boosts. Try it yourself.

2. Point boosts: use them strategically

Where to get them: chests in learning paths (Pre Security, Cyber Security 101, AI Security) and completing all weekly missions.

Boost typeHow I use it
50% or 100% for 30 min, activates next dayPrepare ahead. Solve challenge rooms, write down the answers without submitting, then submit them the next day. Best for rooms you can do on your own computer without a VPN or AttackBox. Examples: Capture the Flag, CTF Collection Vol. 1, ReverseELF.
50% or 100% for 30 min, activates immediatelyShort rooms and quizzes. Don’t waste half the boost deciding what to do.
20% or 30% for 2–3 hours, activates immediatelyLonger study sessions. Pick rooms with many questions that you can work through without rushing the learning.

Extra tips

  • Don’t open every chest right away. Save unopened chests for when you need a boost. You can return to finished paths to collect unopened ones.
  • Watch your streak freezes. Chests and missions can give freezes instead of boosts. Once I had 15 freezes I was guaranteed a boost, but that’s my observation, not a confirmed rule.
  • Plan around weekly missions. If you’re near finishing them, time the last ones so you can use any boost well.

Prepare before you activate a boost, and don’t spend its limited time deciding what to do.

3. League strategies: treat it like a race

What works depends on your time, the competition and your effort. Here are four approaches I’ve tried or seen.

Even-Split: earn a steady number of points every day

EffortConsistent daily
My resultFirst in Diamond
RiskLow

My favourite, because it rewards consistency and keeps you focused on your own progress. Set a daily target that gives you a good chance at first place. Around 500–1,000 points a day can put you in a strong position in higher leagues, depending on that week’s competition. Check the leaderboard and raise your target if someone is gaining much faster.

Why I like it: the effort is spread across the week, you keep a learning routine, and you don’t depend on a last-minute grind.

Sit-and-Pack: stay close, then push ahead at the end

EffortSteady, then a final sprint
NeedsLeaderboard tracking
RiskMedium

Stay within the top three all week. Pick the maximum gap to first place you’re willing to tolerate, work at a steady pace while it holds, then put in the extra effort on the final day to overtake the leader.

Why it can work: you don’t have to outpace everyone from day one, and you save energy for the finish.

Downside: you must keep watching the competition, and the leader may not stop earning points.

Positive-Split: build a big lead early and hold it

EffortHeavy at the start
NeedsLots of free time early
RiskHigher

Earn a lot of points at the start of the week to open a gap. Ideally the lead discourages others and lets you ease off later.

Downside: it worked for others but not well for me. The upfront effort is large, there’s no guarantee it’s enough, and someone may be more determined than you.

Negative-Split: join the league late with a head start

EffortConcentrated near the end
NeedsGood timing
RiskHighest

The unconventional one. As I understand it, you’re assigned to a league after completing two rooms, so you time that to happen one or two days before the week ends. Earlier, work through longer rooms and submit some questions but leave the rooms unfinished until the last day or two. You then join with a point lead while others are just starting.

Why it can work: you compete over a shorter window instead of the whole week.

Verify first. This depends on league assignment and scoring working as expected, and it leaves little room to recover if your timing is off. Check the current league rules, since platform mechanics change.

4. Which strategy should you choose?

If you…Choose
Prefer consistency and want to focus on learningEven-Split
Enjoy competition and don’t mind watching the leaderboardSit-and-Pack
Have more time at the start of the weekPositive-Split
Can control timing and understand league assignmentNegative-Split

Other players’ behaviour is outside your control. You can plan your effort, use boosts well and learn as much as possible, but first place is never guaranteed. I once lost a week with over 5,000 points. And that’s fine.

Final thoughts

Getting first place is satisfying, and I enjoy it. But the leaderboard is only one part of the experience. Just make sure you’re getting something meaningful from all that effort:

  • Learn the concepts.
  • Practise the techniques.
  • Go back to rooms you struggled with and solve them again without the answers.
  • Use walkthroughs when you need them, and make sure you understand what you read.

The best achievement isn’t first place in a league. It’s taking what you’ve learned and using it to solve a problem you haven’t seen before.

And if that helps you land a job in cybersecurity, that’s a badge worth much more than any number on a leaderboard.
